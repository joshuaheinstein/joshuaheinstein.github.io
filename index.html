About

I like the layer where code stops being abstract — where a register write moves something, or a waveform on a scope tells you the truth your assumptions were hiding.

I like the problems that don't arrive pre-scoped — the ones where the first job is figuring out what's actually being asked, and the second is getting a team pointed at it.

I studied engineering at Harvey Mudd, which is a small school with an unusually stubborn insistence that you build the thing yourself. That's where I designed a 32-bit RISC-V processor from an empty file, and where I learned that most of engineering is verification.

Since then I've worked on drone localization at Amazon Prime Air, led a team of six automating a bioreactor process for XDemics, and most recently built vehicle telemetry hardware at an EV company in New York — designing the loggers, manufacturing them, writing the desktop tooling, and taking them into the field.

I'm looking for embedded, firmware, or hardware systems work where I can own something end to end. I'm in New York City.

I'm looking for systems engineering, technical program, or hardware leadership work where the problems are ambiguous and the ownership is real. I'm in New York City.
