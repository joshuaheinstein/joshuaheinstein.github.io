  6. 06 Nov – Dec 2025

    LED Zeppelin — audio in, light out.

    • 512-point FFT via CMSIS-DSP on the MCU, extracting dominant frequencies into a composite square wave.

  7. 07 Design decision

    Why two chips instead of one.

    • MCU does the maths. CMSIS-DSP gives a tuned, well-tested FFT on hardware built for it — no reason to rebuild that in fabric.
    • FPGA does the timing. Windowed sampling, parallel binning, and many simultaneous PWM channels are exactly what fabric is good at, and it's deterministic.
    • The interface is the interesting part. A composite square wave carries the frequency content across on a single line — the FPGA recovers the bands by counting, not by parsing a protocol.

    Trade-off worth naming out loud: that encoding is cheap and glitch-tolerant, but it throws away amplitude information the FFT already had.

    Numbers to confirm before presenting

    sample rate · bin width · window fn · PWM freq · LED count · end-to-end latency

    Re-read the code before quoting any of these. In a deep-dive, “I'd have to check” beats a number from memory.
