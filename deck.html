01 · Root cause

A rogue CAN command from a bad app update.

  • Millions of raw frames reduced to dashboards and metrics engineers could actually read.
  • Correlated shutdown events against bus traffic in the seconds before each one.
  • Isolated a command that shouldn't have been there — traced to a faulty mobile app update, not a hardware fault.

The instrument was the deliverable, but the answer was the point: the fix belonged to a different team, and the data is what told us that.

02 · Sequence and validation

Tuned in simulation, proven on the bench.

How it was proven

  • 2D COMSOL simulations of the fluid behaviour, used to predict surface coverage before touching cells.
  • Empirical testing against the model — simulation and bench together confirmed 100% surface coverage.
  • Parameter tuning on angle and vibration speed, driven by the fluid dynamics data rather than guesswork.
  • Result: >95% recovery in live-cell testing.

03 · Nov–Dec 2025

LED Zeppelin — audio in, light out.

  • 512-point FFT via CMSIS-DSP on the MCU, extracting dominant frequencies into a composite square wave.
  • FPGA samples that wave in 100 ms windows, binning frequency counts into 12 linear bands.
  • Bands drive the RGB display over PWM, with low latency between intake and render.
  • Dual-platform by design — each side doing what it's good at.

03 · Design decision

Why two chips instead of one.

  • MCU does the maths. CMSIS-DSP gives a tuned, well-tested FFT on hardware built for it — no reason to rebuild that in fabric.
  • FPGA does the timing. Windowed sampling, parallel binning, and many simultaneous PWM channels are exactly what fabric is good at, and it's deterministic.
  • The interface is the interesting part. A composite square wave carries the frequency content across on a single line — the FPGA recovers the bands by counting, not by parsing a protocol.

Trade-off worth naming out loud: that encoding is cheap and glitch-tolerant, but it throws away amplitude information the FFT already had.
